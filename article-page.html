Active listening: a better way to connect

Listening well is more than waiting for your turn. Notice the main point, reflect what you heard, and ask a question that helps the conversation move forward.

By EDGE Editorial·October 18, 2026·5 min read
LISTEN / UNDERSTAND 01 HEAR
MORE. MAKE SPACE FOR THE WHOLE THOUGHT

Most conversations move quickly. While someone is speaking, we may already be deciding what to say next, preparing advice, or looking for a gap to jump in. Active listening gives the other person a little more room—and gives you a clearer picture of what they mean.

It does not mean agreeing with everything or staying silent no matter what. It means paying attention long enough to understand the point before you answer. That small pause can make everyday conversations, teamwork, and difficult discussions more productive.

Start by listening for the main point

Give the speaker your attention and notice what sits underneath the details. Are they sharing a problem, making a decision, or asking for support? You do not need to guess perfectly. A short follow-up can help you check what you heard.

Try putting distractions aside, facing the person when it feels natural, and letting them finish a thought. These simple signals make it easier to stay with the conversation instead of rehearsing your response.

Three small moves to try

  1. Pause before you answer.After the speaker finishes, give yourself a beat. The pause helps you respond to what was said instead of the first reaction that came to mind.
  2. Reflect the main point.Use a brief summary in your own words: “It sounds like the timing is the hard part.” This lets the other person confirm or clarify without having to start over.
  3. Ask one open question.Questions like “What would help most right now?” invite the conversation forward. Ask one at a time and leave room for the answer.

“I want to make sure I understand—what feels most important to you here?”

A simple check-in can change the pace of a conversation.

Listening without rushing to fix

When someone brings you a challenge, advice can feel like the fastest way to help. But they may first need to explain what happened or sort out how they feel. Before offering a solution, ask whether they want ideas, a second perspective, or just a listening ear.

If you do share a suggestion, keep it open rather than presenting it as the only answer. “Would it help to think through a couple of options?” respects the other person’s judgment and makes room for collaboration.

When you disagree

Listening carefully does not require you to set aside your own view. First, show that you understand the other person’s point. Then explain where your perspective differs and why. Keeping those steps in order makes disagreement easier to work through without turning it into a contest to be heard.

Active listening is a habit, so it will not feel natural in every moment. Start with one conversation today: pause once, reflect one point, or ask a question before you offer advice. Notice what changes.

The EDGE takeaway

Listen for the point, check what you understood, and ask what would help next. A clearer conversation can start with one thoughtful pause.
